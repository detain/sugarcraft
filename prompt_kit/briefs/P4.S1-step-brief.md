## THE TWO THINGS MOST LIKELY TO MAKE THIS STEP WRONG

Both are in your hazards list; they are repeated here because they are the ones that pass every
naive test.

1. **`toArray()` / `fromArray()` (`src/Usage.php:151-177`) cross a FORK SOCKET.** They exist only
   because `Backend\EngineBackend::completeAsync()`'s parent unserializes with
   `allowed_classes => false` and so cannot receive the object; `EngineBackend.php:1384` rebuilds it
   with `Usage::fromArray($data['usage'] ?? null)`. **A bucket added to the class but not to both
   halves of that pair works perfectly in sync mode and silently reads zero in async mode.** Every
   unit test of the new fields passes. The Done-when needs a **round-trip test**
   (`fromArray($x->toArray())` equals `$x`, bucket by bucket, including the null-vs-zero
   distinction) — not a test that asserts the array has the right keys. And `fromArray()` must stay
   tolerant of a shape it did not write: its docblock says a corrupt frame must cost the turn its
   accounting, not the turn itself. **A frame missing the new keys entirely must not become four
   zeroes** — that is the null-vs-zero contract crossing the socket.
2. **`plus()` (`src/Usage.php:118-121`) adds two fields by hand.** Its own docblock says a turn is N
   provider calls, so a bucket omitted there is dropped on the **common** path. `sum()` is built on
   `plus()` and inherits the bug. Both need tests that would go red if a single bucket were dropped
   from the addition — and per §1.11 that means asserting the summed VALUES, not that the result is
   non-null.

## §1.11 — WHAT COUNTS AS A TEST HERE. READ THIS TWICE.

These do **not** count, and a test file that grows only these has added no coverage:

- Annotations: `@covers`, `@test`, a descriptive method name.
- Existence checks: `method_exists()`, `class_exists()`, `is_callable()`, `property_exists()` — they
  assert something was **typed**, not that it **runs**.
- Shape assertions on the thing under test: `assertNotNull`, `assertIsArray`, `assertIsInt`,
  `assertTrue(count(...) > 0)` — they pass on the **wrong value**.

A real test calls the thing and asserts the **value**, asserts **exact counts**, covers **both
polarities** and the **pathological input**, and **goes red when the change is reverted**.

**You must state, in your final report, the deletion experiment you actually ran** — for each of the
Done-when clauses: the exact edit you made to revert the change, the command you ran, and the test
that went red with its name and count. "It would fail" is not a deletion experiment. Run it, then
restore the file, then say you restored it. An unstated or unrun deletion experiment fails the step
regardless of how green the suite is.
